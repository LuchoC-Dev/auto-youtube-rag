# Evaluation report 3.2 — auto-youtube-rag

## Status

**Closed on 13 August 2026.** Blocks M (mechanical Layer A), N (judged
Layer B) and O (calibration and closing) of
[`docs/eval-design.md`](../../../docs/eval-design.md) are complete. This
document is the final report of point 3.2, the last block left open in the
MVP according to `docs/build.md`.

**Note on the bundles:** the 24 generated bundles (`context.md` and
`result.json` per query and depth) are not versioned in this repository
because they contain verbatim text derived from third-party YouTube videos.
They can be regenerated by running `evals/run-seed-queries.ts` over
`evals/queries/seed-queries.json` against a collection of your own. The
Layer B judgments (`judgments/claude/`, `judgments/codex/`) are kept: they
are the project's own evidence, not third-party content.

**Note on the language of the judgments:** the 48 files under
`judgments/claude/` and `judgments/codex/` are in Spanish, and stay that way
on purpose. They are not documentation — they are the verbatim answers the
two judges gave to a rubric that was itself written in Spanish
(`evals/rubric-template.md`). Putting them through a translator would edit
the evidence of an evaluation rather than translate a document: a judgment
that has been rewritten is no longer the judgment that was given. The seed
query ids used throughout this report stay in Spanish for the same reason —
they are identifiers, and the collection they hit is Spanish.

## Executive summary

3.2 evaluated `retrieve` over a temporary filtered copy of the real
`auto-design` collection (34 videos with a valid `resources.rules`) with the
real E5 model, in two independent layers and with no hand-labelled ground
truth ([`docs/eval-design.md`](../../../docs/eval-design.md)):

- **Layer A (mechanical, M1–M4):** 24 bundles generated (8 seed queries ×
  3 depths). Perfect citation integrity across all 24. `status` matched the
  expectation in 21/24; the 3 divergences are the same `no_answer` query at
  its three depths, a behaviour already known and explained below, not a new
  bug. No `warning` emitted. Budget exhausted in 23/24 combinations.
- **Layer B (judged, N1–N4):** Codex and Claude evaluated the same 24
  bundles with the same rubric, without seeing each other's answers. 9/24
  pairs diverge numerically, but no divergence is attributable to a product
  defect — all nine are explained by severity of criterion or by ambiguity
  in the rubric (`evals/rubric-template.md`), detailed further below.
- **Calibration decision (O1):** M3 and N4 were reviewed together and **the
  defaults are kept unchanged** — RRF `k = 60`, `wText = wVector = 1.0`,
  budgets `focused` 12k / `balanced` 32k / `deep` 64k. There was not enough
  evidence to justify a change. Full reasoning in
  [`docs/decisions.md`](../../../docs/decisions.md), section "Calibration
  decision (O1, point 3.2)".

**MVP conclusion:** the product retrieves and assembles cited context
correctly and consistently across agent providers. The strongest quality
signal 3.2 produced is not a code bug but a real characteristic of the
corpus (see "Actionable findings" below), and it is documented for whoever
operates the collection, not for `src/`.

### Discrepancies with hypotheses

**1–2. `en-concept-visual-hierarchy` — `balanced` (Δprec 0.25) and `deep`
(Δprec 0.30).** Both judges agree on coverage (5) and on `expected.notes`
being satisfied (`si`); they only diverge on how much of what was included
they count as relevant. Claude counts as partially relevant any fragment
that touches typography/hierarchy even tangentially inside a broader trend
catalogue; Codex discounts that same content more firmly. **Hypothesis: a
divergence of severity criterion in `precision_aparente`, not a bundle
defect** — both judges describe the same content (strong core + trend
catalogue noise), they only weigh differently how much to penalise the
noise. It is amplified at `deep` because there is more total volume to
disagree over.

**3–5. `en-multilingual-typography-pairing` — all three depths**
(Δcov 2–3, `match` no/parcial). Claude rates coverage low (2) because only
one fragment of the bundle deals specifically with "font pairing" and no
fragment is in Spanish, so it marks `no` against `expected.notes`. Codex
rates coverage high (4–5) because it considers the general typographic
guidance available to be enough to build a reasonable answer, and marks
`parcial` instead of `no` because it cannot confirm the total absence of the
multilingual crossover with certainty. **Hypothesis: a real ambiguity in the
rubric** about what "sufficient coverage" means — coverage of the exact
subtopic the query asks for (Claude's reading) vs. general coverage of the
topic area that would let an agent build *some* reasonable answer (Codex's
reading). Both judges agree, in the text of `brecha_percibida`, that there
is no Spanish content about typographic pairing in any bundle — this is a
robust signal of a real gap in the collection (see the N2 finding),
independent of how each judge scored it.

**6. `es-concept-brutalism` — `deep`** (`match` si/parcial, with no numeric
difference: 0.30/5 for both). Claude considers that, with the exhaustive
volume of `deep`, the bundle "more than satisfies" `expected.notes` despite
the noise. Codex stays at `parcial` because a high proportion of the bundle
is still non-brutalist content. **Hypothesis: a threshold disagreement**
about how much noise a "yes" tolerates before degrading to "partial" — both
judges describe the same signal/noise balance, they only round it
differently.

**7–9. `multilingual-grid-systems` — all three depths** (`match`
si/parcial, with no large numeric difference). Claude infers that the
multilingual crossover asked for by `expected.notes` is demonstrated by the
simple fact that English sources appear in answer to a Spanish query
(crossover by construction). Codex demands explicit evidence, visible in the
bundle itself, that retrieval crossed languages — something `context.md`
never declares about itself, because by design the product does not annotate
which path each fragment arrived by. **Hypothesis: this is the clearest
discrepancy of rubric ambiguity, not of the bundle** — as it is worded, no
bundle could ever satisfy Codex's stricter reading, because `context.md`
does not expose its own retrieval mechanism. If a future evaluation pass is
run, it is worth clarifying in `evals/rubric-template.md` whether
"multilingual crossover" is accepted on the mere presence of sources in
another language or requires explicit evidence in the bundle.

### Aggregate reading

The 9 discrepancies fall into two categories, neither attributable to a
product defect:

- **Severity of `precision_aparente`** (2 cases): both judges describe the
  same content, they only weigh the thematic noise differently.
- **Rubric ambiguity about "sufficient coverage" and "demonstrated
  multilingual crossover"** (7 cases): the wording of N1 allows two
  reasonable and distinct readings — neither is a misreading of the bundle,
  both judges cite the same evidence and arrive at different interpretations
  of what counts as "satisfied".

No discrepancy indicates that a judge misread the bundle or hallucinated
content — it is consistency of *rubric interpretation*, not of *product
reading*, which confirms that it is the instrument (N1) that has points to
sharpen before a future pass, rather than the product being evaluated.

## Actionable findings

None requires changes in `src/` to close the MVP. They are documented for
whoever operates the collection or continues the product after 3.2:

1. **Schema drift in the real `auto-design` manifest, outside this
   repository.** The collection grew from 34 to 51 videos since the last
   validated `sync` (2.1); 17 of the 51 use `resources.analysis` instead of
   `resources.rules`, which makes the whole manifest fail with
   `MANIFEST_SCHEMA_INVALID` if it is synced without filtering. `sync`
   behaved exactly as designed (the run fails, existing packages are not
   deleted), so it is not a bug — but it blocks syncing the full collection
   today. M4 was run over a copy filtered down to the 34 valid videos.
   Accepting `resources.analysis` as an alias, or coordinating with the
   pipeline that produces the packages so that it stops emitting the old
   key, requires explicit approval before touching
   `src/infrastructure/filesystem/manifest-reader.ts` — it is not decided in
   3.2.
2. **Apparent precision is limited by shared catalogue noise, not by
   retrieval errors.** Almost every seed query retrieves from the same
   subset of ~20 `auto-design` videos about style/trend catalogues
   (2025/2026), so as the budget grows (`focused` → `deep`) more tangential
   catalogue comes in instead of more specific content — Claude reported
   that apparent precision generally **dropped** with depth for this reason
   (N2). This is a characteristic of the real corpus, not of the ranking:
   RRF simply has no evidence left to distinguish "a catalogue that mentions
   the topic in passing" from "specific content about the topic" without an
   additional signal that does not exist today (for example, a unit type or
   a thematic density). Noted as a candidate entry for a later phase outside
   this MVP, not as a 3.2 pending item.
3. **`en-multilingual-typography-pairing` exposes a real gap in the
   collection, not a retrieval defect.** No Spanish video about "typographic
   pairing" exists in the corpus, not even visible at `deep` (99 of 101
   candidate blocks included). Both judges confirm it independently. This is
   information about the `auto-design` collection, not about the product.
4. **`evals/rubric-template.md` has two points of real ambiguity**,
   responsible for 7 of N4's 9 discrepancies: what counts as "sufficient
   coverage" (coverage of the exact subtopic vs. coverage of the general
   topic area) and whether "demonstrated multilingual crossover" is accepted
   on the mere presence of sources in another language or requires explicit
   evidence inside `context.md` itself (which the product never declares, by
   design). Sharpen the wording before a future evaluation pass; it does not
   block closing 3.2 because neither reading indicates that the product
   fails.

## Calibration decision (O1)

Reviewing Layer A (M3) and Layer B (N4) together, **no sufficient evidence
was found to change the RRF weights or the per-depth budgets.** `k = 60`,
`wText = wVector = 1.0`, `focused` 12k / `balanced` 32k / `deep` 64k
estimated tokens are kept, exactly as approved since 2.2/2.3.

Reasons in brief (full detail with the point-by-point evidence in
[`docs/decisions.md`](../../../docs/decisions.md), section "Calibration
decision (O1, point 3.2)"):

- The almost universal budget exhaustion (Layer A table above) is the
  expected result of deliberately retrieving a broad universe of candidates,
  not evidence of undersized budgets.
- Judged coverage flattens from `balanced` to `deep` in most queries with
  real content, and no smaller preset performs worse than a larger one —
  consistent with distinct usage profiles, not with a broken preset.
- `es-no-answer-unrelated-topic` never produces `status: "no_results"`, but
  both judges score it `precision_aparente = 0.00` with no divergence at the
  three depths: the consuming agent reaches the correct conclusion anyway,
  so adding a vector similarity floor — explicitly out of scope for 3.2
  except with clear evidence — is not justified by this run.
- No data from 3.2 isolates the contribution of the textual path against the
  vector one, so there is no signal to move `wText`/`wVector` in either
  direction.
